EVENT 1859-09-14
- Nothing in the articles supports an aurora on 1859-09-14 itself. Every report is about the great storm of Aug 28-29 and Sept 1-2 1859, reprinted or discussed after the fact.
- Supported nights: Aug 28 (Sunday, into Aug 29), about 20 independent accounts (Dallas, Galveston, Philadelphia, Cincinnati, Louisville, Ravenna, SF, Sacramento, Cuba, England, Germany); Sept 1 (into Sept 2), about 8 (Boston, New Orleans, Davenport, Ravenna, California, bark Pride of the Sea at sea).
- Distinct places: England, Germany, Brussels, Cuba/Havana, New Orleans, Galveston, Dallas, Louisville, Evansville, Cincinnati, Philadelphia, Boston, Ravenna, Plymouth IN, Davenport, West Point, California, Victoria, Maui, at sea.
- Southernmost: Lahaina, Maui, 20.9 N (Havana, Cuba, 23.1 N on the mainland side).
- Features: red/crimson colour widely; corona and pyramids near zenith; light bright enough to read print; telegraph disruption and operation without batteries (Boston-Portland, New York-Pittsburgh); multiple nights; seen in the south and in the tropics.
- Verdict: NOT SUPPORTED for Sept 14; no sightings dated near it, only retrospective coverage of Aug 28 and Sept 1-2.
- Best documents of the real storm: 1859-09-14_10 "whole northern heavens again on fire"; 1859-09-14_17 "working with the auroral current"; 1859-09-14_28 "impossible to communicate between the telegraph stations".
