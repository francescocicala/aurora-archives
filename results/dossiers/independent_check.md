# Independent check against raw OCR (results/dossiers/round1, round2)

Method: I read every article in the five event files named below and recounted the reports myself. A reprint of the same account counts once. Quotes are OCR text, lightly trimmed.

## 1. 1860-09-06 (Thursday night, 6 Sep 1860)
- (a) VERIFIED. 1860-09-06_28, New Orleans Daily Crescent, 7 Sep: "last night ... the telegraph rang the fire bells"; "an Opaque blood.red flush, covering large part of the armament due north". The bells were rung by the fire-alarm telegraph. The paper first blamed a prairie fire, then concluded it was the aurora.
- (b) VERIFIED.
  - Charleston: _13, Charleston Mercury reprinted at Anderson SC. The Orphan House bell "tolled an alarm of fire", which was "caused by a most brilliant display of the Northern Lights".
  - Augusta: _08 and _23, Augusta Constitutionalist reprints (one account).
  - Columbus GA: _25. "seen in Columbus, Ga, on the night of the 7th". This is probably the early hours of the same event.
- (c) PARTLY, but close: 22 firm independent reports, 25 if probables are included.
  - The 22 firm reports: Nevada City CA (_01), Evansville (_02), unnamed paper (_03), Macon MS (_06), Marysville CA (_07), Augusta (_08/_23), Cincinnati (_10), North San Juan CA (_11), Dayton (_12), Charleston (_13), NY Tribune (_14), NY Herald (_20/_24), Placer CA (_15), Memphis (_16), St Louis Bulletin (_16), Philadelphia (_18/_32), Davenport (_21), Eaton OH (_22), Boston–NY telegraph (_27/_34), New Orleans (_28), ship at sea (_35), Yorkville SC (_37).
  - The 3 probables: Columbus GA (_25), Abbeville SC "very generally witnessed" (_23), Winchester TN (_38, "one night week before last").
  - Not counted as 6 Sep: _04, _19 and _30 (Saturday 15 Sep); _17, _31 and _33 (August); _00 ("Tuesday", so 4 or 11 Sep); _05 (Memphis, says "9th").
- (d) VERIFIED. _27, with reprint _34 (Richmond Dispatch): "messages were sent between Boston and New York independent of the galvanic battery".

## 5. 1859-08-28 — PARTLY
- New Orleans: VERIFIED in 1859-08-28_05 (NO Crescent, 1 Sep): "The celestial phenomenon witnessed in this city on Sunday night last". The same article reports Mobile as well.
- Havana: there is no Havana or Cuba text in the 1859-08-28 file. It appears in other files:
  - 1859-09-14_08 (Richmond Whig): "Havana correspondent ... on the night of Sunday, August [digit garbled]". The colour is given as "bloodred". The only possible Sunday is 28 Aug, so the date is inferred.
  - 1859-09-14_00, 1859-09-26_12 and 1859-09-30_01 (NO Picayune): "At Havana it made its appearance a few minutes after [nine] o'clock". These give no date.
- So the Havana report for 28 Aug exists, but only in the 09-14 file, and its date depends on reading "Sunday".
