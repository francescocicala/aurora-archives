# Novelty check, second pass: sources not covered before (2026-11-12)

This pass extends `prior_work_49_storms.md` and `loomis_check.md`. It deliberately avoids repeating their sources.

**Status key**
- **STUDIED**: a modern paper is dedicated to the storm.
- **DISCUSSED**: a modern paper discusses the storm in its text, but is not dedicated to it.
- **LISTED**: the storm appears only in a modern catalogue or table.
- **NOT FOUND**: none of the checks below turned it up.

## Headline finding (new)
**Eapen P. E., Girish T. E., Gopkumar G. & Haritha V. G. (2026).** "Investigations on the Extreme Space Weather Conditions During the Years 1841-1877 Using Geomagnetic Observations in Trivandrum, Singapore, Bombay and Madras". *Preprints.org*, posted 9 Jan 2026. Not peer-reviewed. doi:10.20944/preprints202601.0687.v1 (verified via Crossref). https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202601.0687/v1

**What it contains**
- Storm tables built from low-latitude H data, giving ΔH and a "Tvm Dst" estimate:
  - Table 1: Madras, 1846-1851
  - Table 2: Madras and Bombay, 1852-1855
  - Table 5: Trivandrum and Bombay/Colaba, 1855-1877
- A Greenwich storm class column, "GW Class" (A = intense, G = great). The paper attributes it to Maunder 1905 (MNRAS 65:538, doi:10.1093/mnras/65.6.538) and Jones 1955.
- A definition of "ESW" (extreme space weather) storms: "geomagnetic storms with a value of Tvm Dst equal to 300 nT or more".

**Coverage of our storms.** It lists 1860 Sep 6-7, 1860 Aug 6-7, 1851 Sep 3, 1851 Sep 29, 1861 Mar 9-10 and 1866 Feb 20-21, plus many of the remaining storms (table below). Only 1866 Feb and 1860 Mar get a sentence of discussion. There is no aurora analysis apart from the 1872 Bombay aurora.

**Access caveat.** Direct download was blocked (Akamai "Access Denied"). The rows below were extracted through the WebFetch tool from the HTML full text. **Verify them against the PDF before citing.**

**Implication.** The novelty claim must change: "no modern study" still holds, but "no modern listing" no longer does. Our 1860-09 storm is a Greenwich "G" storm with an estimated Dst of about -466 nT in this preprint. This independently supports its intensity, and the preprint does no aurora analysis. Cite it.

## Sources checked this pass (new) and outcome
- **Wagner, Neuhäuser & Arlt 2023**, AN 344:e20220078, doi:10.1002/asna.20220078.
  - OA copies exist (Wiley pdfdirect, db-thueringen derivate 00060660, Wayback snapshot), but all were blocked: Wiley 403, a db-thueringen proof-of-work wall that even headless Chrome could not pass, and Wayback connection resets.
  - The abstract names only March 1716 and 1859.
  - The Crossref reference list suggests the other events: Kassner 1770, Silverman 2008 (1872), Rubenson 1882 / Tromholt (1882), Lovering 1868, Heis 1860 and Dalton 1834.
  - **Event list still unverified**, but no reference points to 1837/1851/1860/1861/1866.
- **Silverman 2006**, ASR 38:136, doi:10.1016/j.asr.2005.03.157: no abstract or full text reachable (Crossref and OpenAlex have no abstract; ADS is not allowlisted).
- **Nevanlinna 2006**, ASR 38:180, doi:10.1016/j.asr.2005.07.076: abstract only (isc.ac mirror). It compares 1859 with the aa era (1960) and names no 1850s-60s storms.
- **Nevanlinna & Häkkinen 2010**, re-downloaded and grepped: annual means only; no daily storm dates for 1851, 1852 or 1860. It points to Veselovsky 2009 (above).
- **Yakovchouk et al. 2009**, Geomagn. Aeron. 49:153, doi:10.1134/S0016793209020030: 1859 only.
- **Viljanen et al. 2014**, SWSC (full HTML): it names only the Carrington storm for 1850-62. Its Fig. 8 plots 1850-62 daily maxima without dates, and it mentions an unnamed ~700 nT/h event in 1862.
- **Ptitsyna et al. (Geomagn. Aeron. 2018)**: a database including Russian aurora observations from 141 stations, 1837-1909. It is data, not a storm study, but it could contain our dates.
- **Beggan et al. 2022**, GDJ (digitizing UK magnetograms): covers 1946 and 2003 examples, none of our dates. **Beggan et al. 2024** and **van Dam et al. 2025** (SWSC 15:9): 1859 only.
- **Other single-event papers**: Hayakawa et al. 2023 ApJ 959:23 and Berrilli & Giovannelli 2022 SWSC (both 1872); Blake et al. 2020 JGR (Rome 1859); Mikhalev & Beletsky 2025 STP (Irkutsk 1859); Wild et al. 2026 Space Weather, doi:10.1029/2026SW005239 (Exeter train, 18 Oct 1848).
- **Vázquez, Vaquero & Curto 2006**, Sol. Phys. 238:405, doi:10.1007/s11207-006-0194-2: its case events are 1770 Tenerife and 1789 Mexico, with statistics only up to 1860.
- **Spanish and East Asian aurora papers**: Carrasco et al. 2018 HGSS (1770), Vaquero & Gallego 2001, Willis et al. 2006, Hayakawa et al. 2015: none relevant.
- **Boteler 2006** (ASR 38:159) covers 1859 only. No Boteler paper on 1851 or 1860 telegraph effects was found.
- **Fritz 1873 Verzeichniss**: no digitized copy found. It surely lists these nights, but it is a 19th-century source, not modern work.
