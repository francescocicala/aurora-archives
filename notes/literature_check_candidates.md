# Literature check: 12 candidate aurora nights (US newspaper finds)

Checked 2026-11-12. Methods: web searches; Lockwood et al. 2025 full text (both halves); Cliver & Dietrich 2013 full text; Silverman's New England catalogue (NASA SPDF, parsed locally); full text of the American Journal of Science (AJS) 1827–1868 on archive.org (grepped); Barlow 1849 (Phil. Trans.); Prescott 1860; Crossref and arXiv title searches. For magnetometer evidence I computed the daily Ak index at Helsinki from FMI files myself. That is my own derivation, not a published result.

Status key: **DOC** = studied as a significant storm in modern literature. **MENT** = appears in catalogues or 19th-century reports only, with no modern study found. **NF** = not found.

## Notes
- **Lockwood et al. 2025** (MNRAS 540:3596, doi:10.1093/mnras/staf827; open copy at https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/539774/) lists 21 events with sightings at QD latitude ≤ 31°. Of our 12 dates, only 1848-11-17 is in the list. None of the others are mentioned anywhere in the text. US sites at 30–38°N geographic were at about 40–49° magnetic latitude in that era, so these events would not have met the paper's cutoff anyway.
- **Hayakawa et al.:** I found no papers on any of our dates. Their 1840s–1860s work covers 1859 (ApJ 2018, doi:10.3847/1538-4357/aae47c; Colaba ApJ 2022, doi:10.3847/1538-4357/ac2601), 1856-10-27 (Philippines), 1872-02 (ApJ 2023, doi:10.3847/1538-4357/acc6cc) and 4 Jul 1860 (Wallace, SWSC 2025, which judged it probably not an aurora). I found no Hayakawa paper on 1847 or 1848.
- **Vázquez et al.** (2006, Sol. Phys. 238:405, doi:10.1007/s11207-006-0194-2, low-latitude aurorae 1715–1860; 2014, arXiv:1309.1502): these are statistical catalogue studies. Neither discusses any of our dates individually.
- **Silverman's New England catalogue:** every date appears there, often with "[LOV]" (Loomis) source tags. https://spdf.gsfc.nasa.gov/pub/data/aaa_special-purpose-datasets/aaa_historical_aurora/new_england_auroral_obs_1720_1948/
- **Helsinki Ak:** Nevanlinna series from FMI (https://space.fmi.fi/MAGN/K-index/HELSINKI/, files Hels_H_K1844-97_05 and Hels_D_K1844-80_06). The ranks are my own (days with Ak 999 excluded). Helsinki is a subauroral station with gappy hourly data, so these are a proxy for storm size, not a Dst value.
- **Not checked directly:** Fritz's 1873 *Verzeichniss* (on e-rara), the Greenwich/Kew printed disturbance lists, and Boteler's telegraph papers beyond 1859.
- **Telegraph sources:** Prescott, *History, Theory and Practice of the Electric Telegraph* (1860), pp. 318–326, transcribed at https://www.earlyradiohistory.us/1860auro.htm. Barlow (1849), Phil. Trans. 139:61.
