# Loomis / AJS check: 19th-century coverage of the target aurora nights

Method: I downloaded the archive.org OCR text (`*_djvu.txt`) for every AJS (2nd series) issue, contents list and index from 1851, 1852, 1860, 1861, 1862 and 1866 (vols 11-14, 29-34, 41-42, plus the cumulative indexes for vols 11-20, 31-40 and 41-50). I also downloaded:
- Trans. Connecticut Academy vol. 1 (1866), which holds Herrick's New Haven auroral register for 1837-1854, Bradley's extracts and Loomis's Stiles notices
- the Smithsonian Annual Reports for the years 1859, 1861, 1862, 1865 and 1866
- Proc. American Academy of Arts and Sciences vols 5 and 7
- Proc. American Philosophical Society vol 7 (no. 64), vol 8 (nos. 65-66) and vol 10

I grepped all of these for aurora/auror, the target dates in several formats, magnetic and telegraph. The text is OCR, so some page numbers are taken from running heads and may be off by one. Downloads are in (local download folder, not included) .

URL pattern for the AJS issues: https://archive.org/details/<identifier> (identifiers are given below).

## 6-9 August 1860 — FOUND (short observational notes; no analysis of a magnetic storm)
1. **E. C. Herrick**, note in "Shooting Stars of August 9-10, 1860", AJS (2) **30**: 296-298 (Sept 1860), Miscellaneous Intelligence. New Haven, night of 9-10 Aug 1860. Quote: "the Aurora Borealis was visible all night, being by turns quite active after twelve o'clock". He adds that it had been seen "with uncommon frequency this summer". https://archive.org/details/sim_american-journal-of-science_1860-09_30_89
2. **F. Bradley and others (Chicago)**, "Further observations on the Shooting Stars of August 9-10, 1860", AJS (2) **31**: 136-137 (Jan 1861). Quote: "The Aurora Borealis was visible Aug. 8, 10, 12, 17, being uncommonly fine" (on the night of the 10th-11th). https://archive.org/details/sim_american-journal-of-science_1861-01_31_91
3. Smithsonian Annual Report for 1861, list of meteorological papers received (around p. 80). Quote: "E. P. Austin ... Aurora noticed at Love Island, Lake Huron, in June, July, and August, 1860." This is only a manuscript listed in the report; the text itself was not published there. https://archive.org/details/annual-report-board-of-regents-smithsonian_1861
- NOT FOUND: any AJS item naming 6 or 7 Aug 1860, or linking August 1860 to magnetic or telegraph disturbance. The only other "Aug. 6, 1860" item is a meteor paper, AJS 33: 338ff (1862). Loomis's 1860-61 aurora articles (5th-8th articles; vol 30 pp. 79ff and 339ff, vol 32 pp. 71ff and 318ff) deal only with the 1859 storm. Loomis's "geographical distribution" paper (vol 30, July 1860) came out before August 1860.

## 6-7 September 1860 — NOT FOUND
Searched: all AJS issues from Sept 1860 to Nov 1862 (vols 30-34) for "Sept. 6", "Sept. 7", "September 6/7(th), 1860" and every "auror" line; Smithsonian Report 1861; Proc. Am. Acad. vol 5; Proc. APS vols 7-8. Every hit for "Sept. 7" in that span is a table value or a dateline unrelated to aurora. I could not get the Smithsonian Report for the year 1860 (the archive.org item labelled `_1860` is actually the report for 1859).

## 29 September 1851 — FOUND
1. **D. Olmsted** (attributed to him in the vol. 12 index), "Aurora Borealis", AJS (2) **12**: 442-443 (Nov 1851). Quote: "three grand auroras, occurring respectively on the nights of the 3d, the 6th and the 29th." He adds that the 29th was seen "as far south as Charleston and Savannah". https://archive.org/details/sim_american-journal-of-science_1851-11_12_36
2. **L. R. Gibbes**, "Aurora Borealis of September 29th, 1851" (extract from the Charleston Evening News), AJS (2) **13**: 128 (Jan 1852). Quote: "witnessed in the Southern States to an unusual extent." https://archive.org/details/sim_american-journal-of-science_1852-01_13_37
3. **J. Le Conte**, "Note on the Aurora Borealis of Sept. 29th, 1851" (Athens, Ga.), AJS (2) **13**: 128-129. Quote: "seen as far south as St. Simon's Island in lat. 31°".
- Herrick's register (Trans. Conn. Acad. 1: around p. 119) has no entries for September 1851: his journal was "not resumed till October" because of illness.

## 3 September 1851 — FOUND (brief mention only)
- Olmsted, AJS (2) 12: 442, the same note as above, lists the auroras of "the 3d, the 6th and the 29th" of September 1851. No dedicated article.

## 18-19 February 1852 — FOUND (several sources)
1. **D. Olmsted**, "Great Aurora Borealis of February 19th, 1852", AJS (2) **13**: 426-429 (May 1852). Quote: "At some of the telegraph stations, the wires were strikingly affected". https://archive.org/details/sim_american-journal-of-science_1852-05_13_39
2. **D. Kirkwood**, "Aurora Borealis of February 19, 1852", AJS (2) **13**: 430. Quote: "Incomparably the grandest auroral display I have ever seen".
3. **G. B. Prescott**, letter printed in Loomis's "Great Auroral Exhibition ... 2d Article", AJS (2) **29**: 92-94 (Jan 1860), at p. 93. It describes a telegraph chemical-paper record made on "February 19, 1852" when a flame "burned through the prepared paper". https://archive.org/details/sim_american-journal-of-science_1860-01_29_85
4. **E. Loomis**, "The Great Auroral Exhibition ... 1859" (8th article), AJS (2) **32**: 324 (Nov 1861). Quote: "The same effect was produced by the aurora of Feb. 19, 1852". The effect meant is the decomposition of potassium cyanide on telegraph paper. https://archive.org/details/sim_american-journal-of-science_1861-11_32_96
5. **E. Loomis**, "On Electrical currents circulating near the Earth's surface ... 9th Article", AJS (2) **34**: 34-45 (July 1862), at p. 42. Quote: "1852, Feb. 19. New Haven. Streamers began to shoot upward... from E. to W." https://archive.org/details/sim_american-journal-of-science_1862-07_34_100
6. **E. Loomis**, "The Aurora Borealis, or Polar Light", Smithsonian Annual Report for 1865, p. 235, repeats the cyanide effect for "the aurora of February 19, 1852". https://archive.org/details/annual-report-board-of-regents-smithsonian_1865
7. **E. C. Herrick**, Auroral Register, Trans. Conn. Acad. **1** (1866), p. 122. Feb. 18: "Not a great display". Feb. 19: "A splendid display, and not often surpassed." https://archive.org/details/transactionsofco16671conn

## 9 March 1861 — NOT FOUND
Searched: AJS vols 31-34 (1861-62), in full, for "March 9", "Mar. 9" and every aurora line; Proc. APS vol 8; Proc. Am. Acad. vol 5; Smithsonian Report 1861. The only "March 9 1861" hit is a non-auroral table value in AJS 32 (July 1861).

## 20 February 1866 — FOUND (outside AJS)
- **J. C. Cresson**, communication at the APS stated meeting of 2 March 1866, Proc. Amer. Philos. Soc. **10**: 206. Quote: "an Auroral display observed by him when at Pottsville ... night of February 20th-21st". The aurora was seen at 2-3 a.m. https://archive.org/details/proceedingsofamer10amer (OCR of the sim_ copy drops the numbers; use this copy).
- AJS vols 41-42 (1866): NOT FOUND. The only aurora items are Farmer, "On the magnetic effects of the Aurora", 41: 118-119, which is about the aurora of Aug 1865, and solar-theory papers.
- Caution: B. V. Marsh, Proc. APS 10: 24ff (March 1865), covers the auroras of 16 January and **20-21 February 1865**. Do not confuse this with the 1866 date.

## Modern studies
I did not find, within this search, any modern paper specifically about the 1860, 1861 or 1866 nights. Two web searches returned only general Carrington/1859 literature, and I did not verify any of those results as relevant.
